Research

My doctoral research asks how the primate brain represents social relationships — not just who someone is, but what they mean to you. Recording from the amygdala and entorhinal cortex while rhesus macaques view familiar and unfamiliar groupmates, I'm testing whether these two regions encode social structure differently, and whether population-level representations track each animal's own social-behavioral relationships with familiar individuals.

For my postdoc, I'm deliberately moving into learning, memory, and reinforcement learning — a different problem from my PhD. I'm looking for a lab doing NHP or human intracranial electrophysiology alongside genuine computational and theoretical work, not just experimental research with computational collaborators. The goal is a distinct future identity: someone who understands both circuit-level learning mechanisms and social cognition — and I plan to keep collecting my own data the whole way there, an NHP scientist who models, not a modeler who used to do NHP.
