About Me

I am a PhD candidate in Biomedical Engineering at Johns Hopkins University, working in Dr. Ed Connor's laboratory at the Zanvyl Krieger Mind/Brain Institute.

My doctoral research investigates how neural populations in the primate brain represent social knowledge, particularly familiarity and relationships among individuals. I combine nonhuman primate electrophysiology, behavioral observations, and population-level analyses to investigate how social information is organized in the amygdala and entorhinal cortex.

More broadly, I am interested in how information is encoded in high-dimensional neural population activity and how neural dynamics give rise to representations and behavior. For my postdoctoral research, I aim to integrate experimental neuroscience with computational and theoretical approaches, particularly by developing computational models of neural population dynamics and testing their predictions against experimental neural data.
